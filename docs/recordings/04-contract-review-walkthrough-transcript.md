# 04 Contract Review: Papyrus full walkthrough

Runtime 4:06. AI processing and typing are shown at speed; everything else is real time.


## [0:00] upload and read

**[0:00]** This is Papyrus, a legal contract review desk. Four AI inspectors read every contract, flag what could hurt you, and draft the fix. Nothing leaves without a lawyer's sign-off.

**[0:11]** A fresh desk: the docket is empty. A client has just sent over a master services agreement as a Word file.

**[0:14]** It goes in exactly as it arrived. It never leaves this machine: the model doing the reading runs on a private GPU lane.

**[0:18]** Papyrus starts reading immediately, and the review narrates itself while it runs.

**[0:22]** Six stages: read the document, split it into clauses, then four specialist inspectors read every clause in parallel: law, risk, the firm's own standard, and the money terms. Then negotiation angles, and a plain-English report.

**[0:27]** Several minutes of real reading, shown at ten times speed.

**[1:12]** The verdict: every clause of the contract listed, clean ones stepping aside as looks standard, and every flagged clause marked your call. The machine proposes. It never disposes.


## [1:17] accept and keep

**[1:23]** The reviewed contract. On the left rail, the risk picture and every finding; in the middle, the clause-by-clause redline. The lawyer works top to bottom.

**[1:27]** The first flagged clause, expanded. Every finding comes in three plain parts: what is wrong, what we would change it to, and what happens if you ignore it. Below, the redline diff of their wording against the proposed fix, and which inspector caught it.

**[1:35]** The lawyer agrees: accept fix. The corrected wording goes into the final document, and the decision lands on the record under their name.

**[1:39]** Next flag. This time the lawyer disagrees with the machine.

**[1:45]** Keep their wording. Papyrus records that a person saw the flag and chose to keep it. The AI proposes, the human disposes, and both kinds of call are first-class.


## [1:49] edit and counsel

**[1:55]** Two decisions are on the record. The lawyer has two more ways to rule.

**[1:59]** On this flag, the lawyer likes neither wording.

**[2:05]** Edit opens the proposed text for rewriting.

**[2:08]** The lawyer writes the wording they actually want, sped up here.

**[2:09]** Use my wording. A third kind of ruling: neither theirs nor the machine's, the lawyer's own.

**[2:13]** And a hard one, where the lawyer wants a second opinion.

**[2:19]** Ask counsel wakes a fifth agent: a senior-counsel reviewer that reads just this clause and rules on what to do with it.

**[2:21]** A minute or two of extra reading, sped up ten times, and the ruling lands right on the open clause.

**[2:40]** Counsel's ruling lands on the clause record, attributed to the agent: the concrete concession to demand from the counterparty, written as the ask itself.

**[2:45]** With that in hand, the lawyer accepts the fix. Four flags, four different human rulings, all on the record.


## [2:50] signoff and export

**[2:56]** Under every review sits the audit trail.

**[3:00]** Every pipeline step, every inspector finding, and every one of the lawyer's rulings, hash-chained in order and verified intact. The machine's work and the human's decisions sit in the same tamper-evident chain.

**[3:07]** Finish review. The contract flips to reviewed, and the deliverables unlock.

**[3:12]** The final redlined document, assembled from the four rulings.

**[3:15]** Download corrected docx hands back the client's own Word file, identical everywhere except the clauses the lawyer changed.

**[3:19]** And the review report: the whole engagement on one printable page, findings, decisions and who made them, ready to save as PDF for the client file.


## [3:28] people

**[3:34]** Who gets to sit at this desk is managed, not open. There is no public signup: the administrator creates every lawyer account by hand.

**[3:41]** A new lawyer joins the team: email, password, role.

**[3:42]** Created, and on the roster next to everyone else. Every account here is one the firm chose to make.


## [3:46] docket close

**[3:49]** Back on the docket, the desk keeps moving.

**[3:52]** The next contract goes in, an NDA this time, and Papyrus starts reading it the moment it lands.

**[3:56]** The closing shot is the docket holding the whole lifecycle side by side: one contract reviewed and signed off with its decisions on the record, one mid-read with the pipeline narrating its progress.

**[4:01]** That is Papyrus. AI inspectors do the reading and the drafting, precedent keeps them honest, and a lawyer owns every decision on the record.
