# Papyrus Compliance Rules Pack

Version 1.0. Owner: Aldergate Consulting Pte Ltd, Legal.
These are the firm's standard contract positions. Cite rules by number, for example: breaks rule 12.
A clause that breaks any rule below must be flagged. A contract that is silent where a rule requires a protection must also be flagged.

## Payment (rules 1 to 5)

1. Payment terms must be net-30 or better: an undisputed, valid invoice is paid within thirty (30) days. Any longer payment period must be flagged.
2. Interest on late payment must not exceed four percent (4%) per year, simple interest. Monthly interest rates and compounding are not acceptable.
3. No more than fifty percent (50%) of total fees may be payable before work begins or goods are delivered. Fully upfront, non-refundable payment is not acceptable.
4. Prices and fees must be fixed for at least the first twelve (12) months. Any change requires at least sixty (60) days written notice and both parties' written agreement.
5. No fee or penalty may be triggered by the company exercising a contractual right, such as querying an invoice, rejecting defective goods, or cancelling within an agreed window.

## Liability and indemnity (rules 6 to 11)

6. Every contract must cap each party's total liability. Unlimited liability for the company is never acceptable.
7. The standard cap is the fees paid or payable in the twelve (12) months before the claim. A cap for the counterparty materially lower than the company's cap must be flagged.
8. Exclusion of indirect and consequential loss must apply to both parties equally. A one-sided exclusion protecting only the counterparty is not acceptable.
9. The company does not give one-sided indemnities. The company must never indemnify a counterparty for the counterparty's own negligence or breach.
10. A counterparty must not exclude its liability for defective goods, defective services, or breach of confidentiality.
11. Fixed penalty amounts and liquidated damages must be proportionate to a genuine estimate of loss and require partner sign-off.

## Termination and term (rules 12 to 16)

12. The company must be able to terminate for convenience on written notice of sixty (60) days or less. A contract the company cannot exit must be flagged.
13. Termination rights must be mutual. A contract where the counterparty can terminate for convenience but the company cannot is not acceptable.
14. Automatic renewal must not require an opt-out notice earlier than sixty (60) days before the renewal date, and a renewal term must not be longer than the initial term.
15. An initial term longer than twenty-four (24) months requires partner sign-off.
16. On termination each party must return or destroy the other's confidential information and data, keeping only what the law requires.

## Confidentiality (rules 17 to 20)

17. Confidentiality must be mutual: both parties' information is protected on equal terms.
18. Confidential information may be shared only with people who need it for the contract and who are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as strict.
19. A party compelled by law to disclose must give the other party prompt written notice where the law allows, and must disclose only the minimum required.
20. Confidentiality obligations should survive between two (2) and five (5) years after the contract ends. Perpetual confidentiality is acceptable only for trade secrets.

## Intellectual property (rules 21 to 23)

21. Work the company pays for belongs to the company: ownership of deliverables must transfer no later than full payment. A revocable licence in place of ownership is not acceptable.
22. Each party keeps its pre-existing intellectual property. A clause giving the counterparty rights over the company's own materials beyond what the work needs must be flagged.
23. Receiving confidential information must never grant the receiver any licence or ownership in it.

## Restraint (rules 24 to 26)

24. The standard restraint is non-solicitation only: no soliciting the other side's involved employees, for at most six (6) months after the contract ends.
25. Non-compete obligations on the company are not acceptable in commercial contracts. In an employment contract a non-compete must be six (6) months or shorter and limited to a named role, market, and geography.
26. A restraint must never prevent the company from serving its own clients or from hiring through general public advertisements.

## Data protection (rules 27 to 30)

27. Any contract touching personal data must require compliance with the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (Singapore) or an equivalent standard, in both directions.
28. Personal data may be used only for the purposes of the contract. Sub-processors require the data owner's prior written consent and equivalent obligations.
29. Personal data must not be transferred outside the agreed jurisdictions without prior written consent and equivalent safeguards.
30. Each party must notify the other of a personal data breach within seventy-two (72) hours of becoming aware of it.

## General (rules 31 to 34)

31. Governing law must be Singapore, and disputes must be resolved in the Singapore courts or Singapore-seated arbitration.
32. Employment terms must meet or exceed the minimums in the Employment Act 1968 (Singapore), including notice and paid leave. Dismissal without notice is acceptable only for serious misconduct.
33. Notices must be valid when sent in writing by email or registered post. A notices clause limited to channels the firm does not use, such as fax, must be flagged.
34. No clause may allow the counterparty to change the contract, scope, or prices unilaterally. Changes require both parties' written agreement.
